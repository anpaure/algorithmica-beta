Algorithms Case Studies

Minimal loops are useful for isolating one hardware effect, but performance engineering becomes interesting when the effects interact. A faster arithmetic kernel may expose memory bandwidth; a compact representation may enable SIMD; a branchless transformation may add enough work to lose on short inputs.

The case studies in this chapter begin with a correct reference implementation, identify the active constraint, and change one layer of the algorithm at a time. Historical measurements are labeled with their machine and workload. Treat them as evidence for the explanation, then reproduce the experiment on the system that matters.

Each optimized implementation keeps its domain attached: alignment, padding, integer ranges, instruction-set support, numerical error, preprocessing cost, and input distribution are part of the algorithm. The aim is not to collect record claims. It is to practice moving from an abstract operation count to a tested program whose dataflow matches the hardware.
